# Validation framework, full chain rerun (started 2026-10-21)

Target: a journal paper (Transportation Research Part A/B level). Every stage is rerun from its inputs, its weaknesses are
diagnosed and fixed at the stage where they arise, and each stage is validated against independent data before its output
feeds the next stage. All of this work lives in `Validation_2026/`; the earlier project folders are read, not changed.

## Principles

1. **Independent benchmarks.** Each stage is judged against data it was not estimated or calibrated on.
   Where a benchmark was used for calibration (e.g. transit ridership for mode-choice constants), that is stated and a
   second, independent benchmark is reported.
2. **Out of sample.** Population: held-out survey households. Land use: calibrate on 2016–2020, test on 2021–2024.
   Activity model: estimate on one half of the travel-survey households, test on the other half. Assignment: counts never
   enter the model; half of the count stations are held out of every network check.
3. **Replications.** Stochastic stages are run with at least 5 random seeds; tables report mean and spread, so that a
   difference is only called real when it exceeds the seed noise.
4. **Nothing is fitted to the validation data.** Model behaviour (trip rates, destinations, timing, routing) is changed
   only when a stage-level test against survey or census data shows a defect. Traffic counts are a test, never a target.
5. **Standards.** Count comparison follows FDOT 2008 / FHWA 2010 ranges (volume-to-count by facility, %RMSE by volume
   group) and UK TAG M3.1 (GEH on hourly flows only). Activity validation follows the TASHA practice (Roorda, Miller &
   Habib 2008): verification on the estimation year, validation on a different year with observed inputs, KS tests on
   time-of-day and duration profiles, replications. Details: `../../I695_pricing/00_research/tasha_validation_review.md`.
6. **Every dimension, county by county.** Each stage is checked in space (county always; PUMA, tract or zone where
   data exists), in time (every year, not only the end year; calibration and test windows reported separately), in
   composition (each attribute's distribution), in joint structure (cross-tabulations), in dynamics (event rates) and in
   flows (migration, commuting, trips between counties). Stage designs: `../2_silo/VALIDATION.md` and the equivalents.
7. **One source of numbers.** Every table in the report, the dashboard and the paper is generated by a script from
   `Validation_2026/data`, never typed in.

## Target years (decided 2026-10-21)

- **2024 = validation year.** It is the latest year with observed data at every stage: MDOT AADT 2024 (2,025 stations
  freshly counted in 2024; MDOT's service, last updated 2026-08-27, has no 2025 values yet), ACS PUMS 2024 5-year,
  LODES 2023 (2024 not released yet), Census population estimates 2024 (and 2025), the 2023 travel survey (one year off, stated). Every stage is validated
  on 2024 before its output moves on.
- **2026 = study year.** SILO runs 2016 → 2026; the 2024 → 2026 step is checked against the Census 2025 county
  population estimates and 2025 county employment (QCEW). The pricing scenarios run on the 2026 population and the 2026
  network. Counts for 2026 do not exist yet; when MDOT publishes them (2027) the 2026 base can be validated the same way.

## The integrated loop (land use ↔ travel), built properly

The three dynamic models are run as one sequence with travel times fed back at every step; no stage ever runs on
travel times older than two years. One orchestration script (`pipeline/run_chain.py`) drives it; every run, its inputs
and its outputs are logged with a hash, so any year can be reproduced.

```
skims(2016)  ← MATSim 2016 base (network 2016, activity model on the 2016 population)
for Y in 2018, 2020, 2022, 2024, 2026:
    SILO  Y-2 → Y                 with skims(Y-2)           (restart from the Y-2 microdata)
    schools Y                     students → schools
    ABIT  Y                       activity plans of the Y population with skims(Y-2)
    MATSim Y                      network of year Y, plans of year Y, 5 seeds for the validation years
    skims(Y)                      AM-peak congested zone-to-zone times from the MATSim result
    inner iteration (validation years 2024 and 2026 only):
        repeat SILO Y-2 → Y, ABIT Y, MATSim Y with skims(Y) until
        max |Δ population by county| < 0.5 % and max |Δ accessibility by zone| < 2 %  (or 3 iterations)
```

- SILO can restart from any year because it writes the full microdata of every year; the script checks that the
  restart files are the ones the previous step produced (hash).
- Transit travel times per skim year come from the MTA schedule of that year.
- Convergence and the size of the feedback effect are reported (population by county and I-695 volumes with and
  without feedback), so the paper can state what the loop changes.
- The pricing scenarios run on the converged 2026 state.

## The network is a validated input, not a given

The road and transit network is built and checked in its own step (`4_matsim/network/`) before any assignment:
- built from a fresh OpenStreetMap extract (the one here is from June 2026); a 2024 version and a 2026 version, differing
  only by documented openings and closures between the two years (Key Bridge closed in both, since March 2024);
- audited link by link against MDOT: functional class, lane counts, posted speeds (OSM maxspeed, MDOT where available),
  interchange geometry on I-695; every difference listed and resolved or stated;
- free-flow and congested speeds checked against NPMRDS (to be obtained for 2024); transit from the MTA GTFS of the
  target year, checked against published headways and ridership.

## Stage gates

No stage output enters the next stage until its validation report in its own folder shows the acceptance criteria met
or the failures stated as limitations with their effect on the next stage quantified. Gate order: population → land use
2016–2024 → network 2024 → activity model → assignment 2024 → land use 2026 → network 2026 → scenarios.

## Figures for the paper (user requirement 2026-10-21)

Every validation figure is a paper figure: produced by a script through `5_reports/paperstyle.py` (one font and size set,
one colour palette with fixed meaning: model blue, observed dark grey, alternative orange; sequential and diverging
ramps for magnitudes and differences; county/period order fixed everywhere), axis labels with units, legends outside the
data, panel letters, PDF and PNG at 300 dpi, single (3.5 in) or double (7.2 in) column width. `paperstyle.finish()` refuses
to save a figure in which any two text elements overlap. Figures are listed in `5_reports/figure_list.md` with their
script, data file and caption, so each can be regenerated.

## Known weaknesses to test and fix, by stage

### 1 VAE population
- Income upper tail and household-level consistency (couple age gap was the accepted trade-off of the simple CVAE).
- Found 2026-10-21 while building SILO's county controls. (a) Households per PUMA match the ACS (0.994) but persons in
  households are 0.936 of the ACS person weights: the VAE learned household frequencies from the household weights, which
  under-represent the people in large households (synthetic mean size 2.52 vs 2.67). Fix: `1_vae/rebalance_population.py`
  re-draws households per PUMA to match both the household count and persons by age band. Group quarters (2.3 % of
  persons) are not synthesized; stated. (b) Placement inside PUMAs used 2016 zone household counts with area-based guesses
  where counts were missing, which misplaced households on the Eastern Shore (Somerset 0.60, Wicomico 0.61, Worcester 1.56
  of the Census). Fix: Census block counts of occupied units per zone, 2010 and 2020 interpolated to 2016 (`1_vae/zone_weights_2016.py`).
- (c) The workplace step read its 2016 job inventory from the last column of the MSTM forecast file, which is the 2040 total:
  2016 jobs were placed by the 2040 forecast. Fixed: LODES 2016 jobs by block mapped to zones (`1_vae/employment_2016_lodes.py`),
  vacancies at the BLS JOLTS Maryland job-openings rate (4.01 % in 2016) instead of the inherited 27 %.
- Tests: held-out PUMS test split (marginals and joint tables per PUMA), SRMSE, bias and precision per attribute; the
  2016 output against ACS 2016 county marginals; worker counts against LODES/ACS by county.

### 2 SILO land use
- DC and WV drift (closed-migration limit), occupation definition gap, race.
- Tests: 2021–2024 ACS per-bin errors (acceptance ±5 pp per bin) for all six states, out of sample; household size,
  income, autos, tenure, workers by county; job totals vs LODES 2023 by county.

### 2b Schools and education trips (new step between land use and the activity model)
- Found 2026-10-21: education trips are absent from the whole chain (the activity model's EDUCATION purpose was off because
  SILO had no schools). Survey: school trips are 8.9 % of Baltimore-region residents' trips, plus parents' drop-off and
  pick-up trips, concentrated in the morning peak on local roads.
- Fix: K-12 school locations and enrollment from the NCES public-school files, colleges from IPEDS; each SILO student
  assigned to a school by age, distance and capacity (script in `2_silo/schools/`), the school zone carried into the
  activity model; EDUCATION tours and ACCOMPANY (drop-off/pick-up) trips switched on and calibrated to the survey.
- Tests: enrollment by school district vs NCES; school-trip rate per student, trip length and mode (incl. school bus share)
  vs the survey; morning-peak profile at the permanent counters before/after.

### 3 ABIT activity model
Found 2026-10-21 (details `../../I695_pricing/00_research/commute_distance_check.py`):
- Workplaces: share of workers employed in their own county is 48 % (from LODES) vs 58 % in the 2017/18 survey and
  ~58–62 % in ACS county-to-county flows. Fix: county-to-county commute flows from ACS/survey; LODES only for the
  within-county distribution.
- Work-trip mix: direct home–work trips are 69 % of work trips vs 53 % in the survey; short work-related trips
  (lunch, errands, drop-offs) are under-generated. Fix: stop-frequency and work-based-tour calibration to the survey.
- Earlier residuals: walk share −3 pp, PM peak −3 pp, transit matched to ridership (calibration, not validation).
- Tests: estimation half vs validation half of the survey; tours and trips per person by purpose, mode shares by purpose
  (car, passenger, transit, walk, bike), trip-length distribution by purpose (KS), departure and duration profiles by
  hour (KS, within 2 pp per hour), stops per tour, same-county work share, commute distance distribution; 10 runs.

### 4 MATSim assignment
Found 2026-10-21:
- Non-freeway network had one flat speed per class and no posted limits; MDOT road classes differed from OSM for
  14,114 links. Fixed in `I695_pricing/data/network_v8_supply.xml.gz`; residents-only runs on old and new network
  are in progress (100 iterations each).
- Transit buses were counted ten times in link volumes (sample expansion applied to buses). Fixed in
  `validation_core.py`.
- Freeways carry traffic the model does not contain (trucks 7–8 % of counts, through traffic, visitors): reported as
  scope, with a passenger-vehicle comparison alongside.
- Tests: daily counts by facility and volume group (half held out), hourly permanent counters (GEH), screenlines,
  travel times on I-695 and radials (NPMRDS, to be obtained), convergence, 5 seeds.
