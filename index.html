    MILES · Maryland Integrated Land-use and Everyday-travel Simulator

    From census records to cars on the road¶

    MILES (Maryland Integrated Land-use and Everyday-travel Simulator) is an agent-based modelling platform for Maryland. It synthesises every household of the region, moves it through the housing and job markets year by year, plans each person's weekday, and simulates the resulting traffic on the road and transit networks.

    Why MILES¶

    • Every person is an agent

      Households, persons, dwellings, jobs and schools are simulated individually, so results can be split by income, age, place of residence or any other attribute.

    • Deep generative population

      A conditional variational autoencoder generates households beyond the combinations seen in the sample, with PUMA and household size as conditions.

    • Land use that evolves

      Residential moves, job changes, births, deaths, marriages and car ownership update the population every year from 2016 to 2024.

    • A full weekday for everyone

      MILES-Day draws activities from survey distributions, schedules them, locates them with estimated logit models and chooses tour modes with household car allocation and joint tours.

    • Dynamic traffic

      MATSim routes every trip and shifts departure times on an OpenStreetMap network with the region's transit timetables and income-based values of time.

    • Estimated and tested, not tuned

      28 choice models estimated with Biogeme; every stage tested on data it never saw. Traffic counts play no role in estimation or calibration.

    How it works¶

    Each stage hands its output to the next. Land use runs year by year; activities and traffic are simulated for a weekday of 2024. Full model design

    What you can do with it¶

    • Road pricing

      Toll schemes on the Baltimore Beltway (I-695): who pays, who diverts, and where traffic goes. Case study

    • Transit service

      Changes to timetables or routes, with door-to-door transit times feeding mode choice.

    • Land-use change

      New housing or jobs in a zone, and how households and workers re-sort over the years.

    • Equity analysis

      Effects by household income, car ownership or home location, person by person.
