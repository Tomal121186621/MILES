    Synthetic population¶

    The 2016 population is compared with data the generator has not seen: the held-out test split of the PUMS 2012–2016 sample, 10 % of the households of every PUMA (25,298 households, 62,467 persons) set aside before training and never used to fit or stop it. The comparison covers all 96 PUMAs and is weighted by the PUMS weights. The tests are joint distributions of two and three attributes, the combinations of attributes the model generates, and each Maryland county against the ACS PUMS 2014–2018.

    The population compared is the 2016 base that enters the model chain. Household counts by PUMA and size are inputs of the generator, and households are redrawn so that persons by age band, and employment by age and sex, match the ACS (model design); those one-way distributions are therefore controlled, not predicted, and they follow the ACS 2014–2018 rather than the 2012–2016 sample. The joint distributions are not controlled.

    Measures, computed on the cell shares of each table (all cells of the cross-classification, empty ones included):

    • SRMSE, standardised root mean square error: the root mean square difference of the cell shares divided by the mean cell share.
    • TVD, total variation distance: half the sum of the absolute differences of the shares, in percentage points of the population.
    • Hellinger distance: \(\sqrt{1-\sum_k \sqrt{m_k o_k}}\), between 0 (equal) and 1 (disjoint).
    • R²: one minus the sum of squared differences over the total sum of squares of the held-out shares (fit to the 45° line).

    Two references are given with each measure. Training sample: the same measure for the training split (80 % of the sample) against the held-out split; it shows how far two samples of the same population differ, mostly through the sampling error of the held-out split. Independent attributes: the product of the training marginals, a table without any association between the attributes.

    Combinations¶

    A household type combines size (1–6+), cars, household income in 22 bands, dwelling type, tenure and workers; a person type combines five-year age band, sex, race and ethnicity, employment in five classes, personal income in 16 bands and relationship (single, married, child). A copy of the sample can only reproduce combinations of the training sample. Held-out combinations absent from the training sample (sampling zeros) test whether the model generates plausible households it has never seen.

    Structural zeros, combinations that cannot occur, are checked with logical rules. Before the generator imposes its five rules on age (see model design), 7.3 % of the decoded persons break one of them; after, none. The employed persons under 16, all aged 15, come from the later adjustment of employment by single year of age, not from the generator.

    Counties¶

    Every Maryland county against the ACS PUMS 2014–2018. The PUMS identifies PUMAs, not counties: a county is compared with the PUMS records of its PUMAs weighted by the county's share of their zones, so a county that shares a PUMA with its neighbours is compared with that PUMA (Garrett and Allegany; Calvert and St. Mary's; the five upper and the three lower Eastern Shore counties). The PUMS samples of the counties' PUMAs range from 1,798 to 19,679 households. The six study counties are at the top, Maryland at the bottom. More county comparisons are in the county view.
