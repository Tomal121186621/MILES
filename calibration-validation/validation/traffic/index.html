    Traffic¶

    The 2024 assignment against the MDOT SHA counts (average annual weekday traffic, AAWDT), the FHWA continuous count stations (TMAS), the screenlines and the MDOT SHA mileage. The assignment carries only the residents' cars with a tour touching the six counties: trucks, commercial vehicles and through traffic are not simulated, so ratios below 1 are expected, most of all off-peak and on roads with much freight. No count was used to set any speed or capacity. Model: mean of five assignment seeds.

    0.91on I-695, passenger vehicles

    How to read the figures

    Orange: model; dark grey: counts. Bands and error bars: range of the five assignment seeds. Ratios are model ÷ count.

    Counts at every station¶

    Stations are matched to network links by road identity; 3,437 of 3,797 stations are matched unambiguously, 2,308 of them on the mainline. Every interstate station, including the Baltimore Beltway (I-695) and the Capital Beltway (I-495), is listed in Interstates by station.

    Across stations the modelled volumes follow the counts with R² = 0.89 and a slope of 0.78 for all vehicles and R² = 0.90 and a slope of 0.84 for passenger vehicles. Two random halves of the stations give the same ratio (0.77 and 0.77).

    On I-695, other freeways and principal arterials the model carries 0.84 to 0.93 of all vehicles and 0.88 to 0.96 of passenger vehicles, and principal arterials are within their tolerance; on minor arterials and collectors it carries 0.58 to 0.69. Only the volume group below 5,000 vehicles a day meets the %RMSE guideline.

    The commute peaks, when residents' travel dominates, are reproduced; the gaps lie where the unmodelled trucks, commercial vehicles and through traffic carry more of the traffic. Neither a class-based coding of speeds and capacities (all-class ratio within 0.01) nor matching stations by proximity alone (ratios by class within 0.03) changes the result.

    Weekday passenger-vehicle miles per resident, 2024. By the residents: car-driver distance of the executed trips of the county's residents wherever driven, against the travel survey 2017/18 (weighted). On the roads: all modelled car traffic on the network of the county, against the MDOT SHA vehicle miles.

    The residents drive slightly more than in the survey, while the traffic on the roads falls short: the gap lies in traffic the model does not carry. The simulated car trips drive 74 % of their route on secondary, tertiary and local roads when start and end lie less than 1 km apart and 14 % when they lie more than 20 km apart. For short trips, 0.2 to 5 km apart, the simulated route is 4 % longer than the fastest route on empty roads and 9 % longer than the shortest route.

    Trip lengths¶

    Measured alike, between census tracts, the residents' car-driver trips average 11.4 km against 11.5 km in the survey, with a TVD of 4.6 percentage points; trips to or from shopping are longer than in the survey (7.6 against 6.6 km) and trips to or from work shorter (15.9 against 17.2 km).
