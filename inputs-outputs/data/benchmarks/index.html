    Validation benchmarks¶

    The data on this page are used only to test MILES; no model is estimated on them. They describe travel and commuting in the forecast year 2024 (the time-use survey, the 2024 American Community Survey) or give an independent check of the base period (the National Household Travel Survey). The ACS microdata used for estimation and calibration are described on ACS PUMS. Bracketed numbers refer to the sources of the data page.

    Shares and means are weighted with the survey weights. Cells based on fewer than 10 unweighted records are shown as "—". The study area or Baltimore region is Baltimore City and the counties of Anne Arundel, Baltimore, Carroll, Harford and Howard.

    American Time Use Survey¶

    The American Time Use Survey (ATUS) 17 records one diary day (4:00 to 4:00) of one person aged 15 or over per household, sampled from households leaving the Current Population Survey. MILES uses the multi-year files 2003–2024 (respondent, activity, summary and CPS files), public, downloaded from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The files hold 252,808 diaries of 2003–2024. They test the number and type of activities per person and the commute in 2024 (activity/lib/reporting_2024/bench_atus.py).

    Processing¶

    • Diary days on Monday to Friday that are not holidays, respondents aged 15 or over living in Maryland (pool Maryland) or in DC, Maryland or Virginia (larger pool, used where the Maryland sample is small).
    • The diary is cut into stays at every travel episode (activity code 18xxxx). A stay after a travel episode is an activity at the trip's destination; the first stay of the day is not an activity. A stay is at home when its episodes are at home or in the yard (TEWHERE 1); episodes without a place take the place of the previous episode.
    • The type of a stay away from home is the category with the most minutes: work (05), education (06), shopping (07) or other.
    • The commute is the last trip leaving home before the first work stay. Its travel time is the sum of the travel episodes up to the arrival at work, without stops; its mode is that of the trip arriving at work.

    National Household Travel Survey¶

    The National Household Travel Survey (NHTS) 18 is the national one-day travel diary of the Federal Highway Administration. Two public files are used:

    • NHTS 2017 (household, person and trip files): an independent check of the base period of the regional survey, for residents of the Baltimore–Columbia–Towson metropolitan area (activity/lib/reporting_2024/obs_surveys.py). The national file holds 129,696 households, 264,234 persons and 923,572 trips.
    • NHTS 2022 (trip file): the trip length of car drivers and the share of business driving, used to check the traffic assignment (assignment/validation/regional_validation.py, coverage_estimate.py). The public 2022 file does not identify the metropolitan area; the closest class is the South Atlantic division, metropolitan areas of 1–3 million with heavy rail. The national file holds 31,074 trips.

    Processing¶

    • NHTS 2017: households of the Baltimore–Columbia–Towson area (which adds Queen Anne's County to the study area) with a weekday travel day; persons aged 5 or over. Legs that end in a change of mode (WHYTO 7) are joined to the next trip. Destination purpose: home (1, 2), work (3), school (8), shopping (11), other. Distances are capped at 300 km.
    • NHTS 2022: weekday car-driver trips (DRVR_FLG 1, TRPTRANS 1–4) with a positive distance and duration.

    American Community Survey 2024, journey to work¶

    The journey-to-work questions of the ACS person file 1 describe commuting in the forecast year: whether a worker worked from home, the mode, the departure time and the travel time to work on the reference week. MILES uses the Maryland person files of the 2024 1-year sample (62,777 records) and of the 2020–2024 5-year sample (299,422 records), both public (activity/lib/reporting_2024/bench_acs.py). The same file series is described for estimation on ACS PUMS.

    Processing¶

    • Workers: ESR 1, 2, 4 or 5 and age 16 or over. Counties are assigned from the 2020 PUMA with the PUMA-to-county lookup of the 2024 code list; in the study area every PUMA lies in one county.
    • The departure time is the midpoint of the JWDP interval. Mode: drove alone counts as driver, a car-pool of k persons as 1/k driver and (k − 1)/k passenger, motorcycle as driver; subway, commuter and light rail as rail; ferry, taxi and other as other.

    American Community Survey summary tables¶

    Published ACS estimates 1 (detailed tables, with 90 % margins of error) are the benchmark where the microdata are too coarse: by county for commuting and by census tract for households and housing. They were downloaded from the data.census.gov table service as one JSON file per table and county (60 county files; 114 tract files).

    • County tables, 2024 1-year and 2020–2024 5-year (assignment/validation/regional_validation.py): B08134 (travel time to work by mode), B08136 (aggregate travel time), B08301 (means of transportation), B08302 (departure time) and B08303 (travel time) for the six study-area counties. Tables the Census Bureau does not publish in the 1-year release are empty: B08134 Carroll, B08134 Harford, B08134 Howard, B08136 Carroll, B08136 Harford, B08301 Harford.
    • Tract tables (reports/validation_tracts/tract_validation.py): 2012–2016 5-year on 2010 tracts (B08202, B11001, B19001, B25003, B25007, B25008, B25009, B25032, B25044) and 2020–2024 5-year on 2020 tracts (B01003, B08201, B08301, B11001, B19001, B19013, B25003, B25008, B25032, B25044), all tracts of the six counties.

    Processing¶
