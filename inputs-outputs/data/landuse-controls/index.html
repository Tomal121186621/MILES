    Land-use controls and county attributes¶

    These county-level data hold the land-use model to observed totals (population, new dwellings, housing units), describe counties in the residential location model (school quality, crime), convert dollars between years, and serve as validation targets (commuting and migration flows). All are public. Counties marked * form the study area.

    Population estimates and components of change¶

    Annual county population estimates of the Census Bureau, July 1 of each year [5]: the 2010–2020 intercensal estimates (by age and sex) for 2016–2019 and vintage 2025 (co-est2025-alldata.csv) for 2020–2025, so that the whole series rests on the 2020 Census. The population control of each land-use region in year Y is its synthetic 2016 population times the region's growth from 2016 to Y; 2026 extrapolates the 2024–2025 growth. Births and deaths of the components of change are validation targets for the demographic models (vintage 2020 up to 2020, vintage 2025 after).

    Processing¶

    • Counties are summed to the 44 land-use regions; a county split across regions is shared in proportion to its zones. Virginia composite codes of the zone system are expanded to their Census counties (51919, 51942, 51951).
    • Control of region r in year Y = synthetic persons of r in 2016 × PEP(r, Y) / PEP(r, 2016).
    • Validation: births per woman aged 15–50 and deaths per person, Maryland, from the components of change.

    Redistricting data: housing units by block¶

    Table H1 (occupancy status) of the 2010 and 2020 Census redistricting files (PL 94-171) for Maryland blocks [2]. Occupied units place synthetic households in zones within their PUMA; vacant units give the vacant dwelling stock of the 2016 base. Block locations: TIGER/Line 2010 block internal points (2010) and the LODES crosswalk (2020). The 2020 files of the District of Columbia, Virginia and West Virginia are also held but are not used in the base year.

    Processing¶

    • 2010 blocks are read from the fixed-width geographic header (summary level 750) and segment 2 of the file.
    • Blocks are located by their internal point and joined to the zone polygons; points outside every polygon take the nearest zone.
    • Units of 2016 = units 2010 + 0.6 × (units 2020 − units 2010), by zone; the same rule gives vacant units.

    Building Permits Survey¶

    New privately owned housing units authorised by building permits, by county and year, annual county files (co<year>a.txt) [7]. Maryland counties build each year's permitted units in the land-use model: one-unit permits go to single-family detached, attached and mobile homes by the model's demand, 2–4 units to small multifamily and 5+ units to large multifamily. Permits also bound the land each county needs for new dwellings. The nine files hold 27,308 county rows (all states); Maryland has 24 counties in every year.

    Processing¶

    County-to-county commuting flows¶

    Workers aged 16 and over by county of residence and county of work, ACS 2016–2020 five-year estimates, Table 1 [8]. Used to validate the home-county and out-of-state shares of Maryland workers in 2024, and as the third constraint of the gravity model that assigns workplaces to workers in Pennsylvania, Delaware and West Virginia in the 2016 base.

    Processing¶

    • Fixed-width lines; rows whose earlier residence is abroad, and flows shown as ., are dropped.

    Stanford Education Data Archive¶

    County estimates of test-score achievement and learning, pooled over 2009–2018, cohort-standardised scale, version 4.1 [9]. The residential location model uses the learning-growth estimate (cs_mn_grd_eb) of each county, averaged to land-use regions with assessments as weights, as a school-quality attribute for households with children. Mean achievement (cs_mn_avg_eb) was tested and is not in the final model.

    Crime by county¶

    Part I index offences reported under Uniform Crime Reporting, 2016. Maryland: county table of the Maryland Statistical Analysis Center (1975 to present) [10]; other states: FBI Crime in the United States 2016 tables and state reports. The crime index of a county is its rate of violent and property offences per 100,000 residents (arson excluded) divided by the highest rate in the zone system (Baltimore City); it enters the residential location model for households with children and the region utility.
